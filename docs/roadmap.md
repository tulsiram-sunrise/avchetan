The hub's request log showed why: pushes arrived within 1.5 s of a capture, and the desktop polled every 59–61 s even with the dock hidden for 10 minutes, but Android pauses the app's timers while it is off screen. A change waiting out the 2 s push delay stayed on the phone until the app was opened again, and polls stopped too. The phone now pushes when it leaves the screen and when a change lands while it is off screen, and both apps poll every 30 s. ## v0.5.0 — Tasks, routines and reminders

**Planned in detail 2026-11-16:** spec [tasks, routines and reminders](product/tasks-routines-reminders.md), 18 days below (17, plus one added 2026-11-17 for edits made on two devices); decisions in [R25](open-questions.md#resolved) (each device chooses whether it rings; a few minutes' leeway with exact timing as an opt-in; full calendar repeat rules; a short starter list; the AI suggests a chore match and code ticks it).

Agreed 2026-11-10 as the release after v0.4.0. Reminders at a time and repeating tasks, on the phone first:
- Reminders are scheduled with Android's alarm system as local notifications (as the brief reminder is), so nothing runs in the background to wait for them.
- Tasks get a reminder time and a repeat rule (daily, weekly on chosen days, monthly): a schema change in both dialects and sync. Finishing a repeating task makes the next one with a derived ID, so two devices make it once. The repeat rule is the one routines use (below), so there is one repeat system.
- "Remind me at 4 pm to call the dentist" is read by extraction (a new prompt version with fixtures); a reminder can also be set by hand.
- Also from the audit: `/done` and `/undo` on the phone, reusing the fuzzy command matching.

Added 2026-11-11 (the user's points, decided one at a time):
- **Done with a note.** Finishing a task takes an optional note on how it went; **Partly done** records a percentage and keeps the task open. Ahead or behind is computed from `completed_at` against `due_date` ("2 days late"). Tasks get `completion_note` and `progress_pct`.
- **Routines: a regular schedule and daily chores.** One new synced `routines` table with the repeat rule and an optional time window, in two kinds: *assumed* (sleep, work hours: counted as happening unless an entry says otherwise) and *confirm* (chores such as a walk or watering plants: ticked off). Each day of a routine is one row with a derived ID from the routine and the date. On first use the app offers a starter list of chores to pick from (derived IDs from the starter key, so two devices don't make them twice); the user adds, edits and deletes freely. Confirmation is one checklist card on the day plus one evening notification, never one per chore; a logged activity that matches a chore ticks it. Repeating tasks share the repeat rule. An ADR decides the table shape and how occurrences are made.
- **The brief marks what was missed.** The daily brief gets a *Missed or behind* part: tasks due and not done, tasks only partly done, chores not confirmed, assumed routines an entry contradicts. Computed in code like the brief's other facts; the AI only phrases them. No schema change.
- **Day coverage** (a setting, off by default). Hours accounted for out of 24: logged `duration_min` plus assumed routines (sleep, work), with 15% of the day allowed for small things nobody logs. The rest is shown as *unaccounted*, not *wasted*, because an unlogged hour may have been rest. It relies on routines and on durations being right (Phase 8's metric editing), so it comes last.

Added later on 2026-11-11 (second round):
- **Finishing a task** grows from *done with a note*: done (with a note), partly done, **move** (Tomorrow · Next week · Pick a date…, on every task row on both apps) and **cancel** (`cancelled_at`: shown struck through, never counted as missed; **Delete** stays for "this wasn't a task"). Deletes in the main window show an undo message instead of asking *Are you sure?*; on the phone, swiping a task row right finishes it and left moves it to tomorrow.
- **The brief's first two lines:** *Moved forward* and *Friction* become one line each, **✓ Went well** and **! Needs attention**, at the top, with a mark and a colour; *Missed or behind* (above) becomes part of **Needs attention**.
- **Share to Avchetan:** Android's share sheet sends text or a link from any app into a capture.
- **Accessibility pass:** text size follows the system, visible keyboard focus, screen-reader labels, on both apps.

Added 2026-11-15 (from the v0.4.0 test build):
- **Sleep hours.** Extraction stores when an activity happened in `occurred_at`: a stated time ("woke up at 6:05"), or the time it was saved when the words say it is happening now or just happened ("now going to sleep", but not "ran this morning") (a new prompt version with fixtures; the model gives a local time, code turns it into UTC), "woke up" always becomes an activity, and a night's sleep is paired from going to bed and waking up into the `sleep_h` metric, shown in Trends. It fits with the *assumed* sleep routine above. In v0.4.0 nothing is stored yet: Ask sees when each entry was saved and judges from the words whether that is when it happened.

Added 2026-11-17 (from an outside review of the docs):
- **Edits made on two devices keep both versions.** Today, when a task is changed on the phone and on the PC before they sync, the newer whole row wins and the other change disappears without a word ([sync protocol](architecture/sync-protocol.md#conflict-resolution-lww)). v0.5.0's task actions (move, cancel, partly done, notes) make that likelier. LWW stays as it is, so every device still ends up the same; the version that lost is kept on the device that wrote it, in a new **device-local, unsynced** `sync_conflicts` table, and the user sees *Changed on two devices* with **Keep this one** (which writes it again as a new edit) or **Keep the other**. A conflict is noticed in two places: the server's push answer marks an acked row that lost (`lost: true`, an additive field, so older apps ignore it), and a pull that overwrites a row still waiting in the outbox. Only user edits are kept (not rows written by extraction), and only when a field the user can see differs. An ADR decides the table and the notice. The same day adds a **randomized convergence test** (`fast-check`, MIT): random edits, deletes and offline gaps on two or three simulated devices must always end with identical data and every lost user edit recorded.

Added 2026-11-18 (from a full architecture review, [R27](open-questions.md#resolved)):
- **Sync keeps working when a newer device sends a value it doesn't know.** Today the wire schemas check every enum column strictly (`packages/sync/src/protocol/rows.ts`), a pulled row that fails fails the whole page (`engine.ts`, `toRemoteChange`), and a hub rejects a pushed row it can't read, which then sits in the outbox. So the first new enum value (a new entry source, a new routine kind) would stop an older phone, PC or hub from syncing until it is updated. The same holds for the repeat rule stored in `routines.repeat_rule`, which the wire checks with the strict `repeatRuleSchema` (`rows.ts`), so a rule with a new field or frequency would break older devices too. The fix only protects devices that already have it, so it ships in v0.5.0 and **v0.5.0 adds no enum value to a synced column and no new repeat-rule field** (Share to Avchetan saves `mobile_text`); the first new value can come in v0.6.0. Leaning, for the ADR to decide: wire schemas take any string for enum columns and any object for the repeat rule, and code narrows when reading (an unknown value shows as a neutral fallback and a rule it can't read makes no days, never a crash) (an unknown value shows as a neutral fallback, never a crash); the Postgres CHECK constraints on synced enum columns go, so a hub stores and passes on values it doesn't know; a row that is truly invalid (a bad ID or timestamp) is set aside and counted instead of failing its page, without moving past it for good. A test pulls and pushes a row with an unknown value. The same ADR also records the other half of compatibility already known in the [sync protocol](architecture/sync-protocol.md#schema-evolution): an older app that pushes a row resets columns it doesn't know to their defaults, so the rules for old and new apps live in one place. [Schema evolution](architecture/sync-protocol.md#schema-evolution) gets the rule.
- **One home for logic both apps share.** Ask, the brief and the command handlers decide "the hub or this device, which model, what if offline" in each app (`apps/desktop/src/services/{ask,brief,commands}.ts` and the phone's copies), because `core` can't import `sync`. Core gets a small `HubClient` interface (ask, brief, command, search) that `@avchetan/sync` implements and the apps pass in, as platform adapters are passed in today; the package rules don't change. `askQuestion`, the brief and `readCommand` move into core with it, plus one `llmDepsFor()` instead of the model wiring repeated in eight files. Then, before the phone gets `/done`: the platform-free command handlers (`done`, `undo`, `brief`, `sync`, `find`) and **one undo model** move into core (`settings`, `lock` and `unlock` stay per platform), and the write actions (the desktop's `ACTIONS` and the phone's `queries/writes.ts`) become shared functions in core, with metric normalization on a hand edit done by the repository. [Command registry](patterns/command-registry.md) is brought in line.

## v0.6.0 — Security

Agreed 2026-11-10 as the release after v0.5.0; to be planned in detail before it starts ([Q29](open-questions.md), [Q6](open-questions.md)):
- **Encrypted hub traffic on home Wi-Fi**, where today plain HTTP is allowed after the *trust this home network* warning. Standard, audited cryptography only (no home-made protocol). Leaning: request bodies encrypted with a key exchanged through the pairing QR (XChaCha20-Poly1305, `@noble/ciphers`), signed requests so the token never travels; a new protocol version and re-pairing. Alternative: HTTPS on the LAN with the hub's certificate pinned through the QR. An ADR decides.
- **A key for each device** (added 2026-11-17, from an outside review). Today every device shares one sync token, so the only way to shut out a lost phone is to rotate it and pair every device again. Since the new protocol version already re-pairs every device, each pairing gives that device its own key (only its hash is kept on the server), tied to its `deviceId`. **Settings → Hub & sync → Devices** lists each device with when it last synced and has **Remove**, which stops that key at once; the others keep working. Pairing codes expire after a few minutes. Same ADR as the traffic encryption. With a key per device, the hub also stops trusting the pushed `updated_by_device` and takes it from the device that signed in, and `origin_device_id` can't change after a row is made (added 2026-11-18, [R28](open-questions.md#resolved): today any token holder can win every tie and delete any row).
- **Encrypted backups and exports** first, then the **PC's database** with SQLCipher (rusqlite's SQLCipher build, key in the Windows Credential Manager, a one-time conversion). The phone's storage is already encrypted by Android; the hub's PGlite can't be, so the guide keeps recommending BitLocker there.
- **Backups to a folder you choose** (added 2026-11-17, [R26](open-questions.md#resolved)). The encrypted daily backups can also be saved to a folder picked in **Settings → Your data** (a OneDrive or Google Drive folder, a USB drive), so a copy lives off the device; on Android today's copies sit in app storage and go with an uninstall. Each new backup is checked by opening it.
- **Faster Ask from the phone** (added 2026-11-17, [R26](open-questions.md#resolved)). When the phone asks the hub, the hub's Ollama runs on the CPU and each tool step is another full pass over a growing prompt. First measure each step on the hub (durations only, never content), then let code gather the likely entries up front (search plus the recent days) so the model answers in one or two passes instead of up to `MAX_STEPS`.
- **"You wrote" apart from "it looks like"** (added 2026-11-17, [R26](open-questions.md#resolved)). Ask, the daily brief and the weekly review keep what an entry says (linked) apart from the model's own reading, and say so when the entries don't show enough. A prompt rule with the citations Ask already returns; no schema change. Done together with the faster Ask, so the prompts are reworked and tested once.
- **The server's own device keeps its copy on disk** (added 2026-11-18, [R27](open-questions.md#resolved)). The server device ([ADR-0021](decisions/0021-server-organizes-as-a-device.md)) keeps every synced row in an in-memory SQLite database (`apps/server/src/fallback/node-sqlite.ts`) and pulls everything again on every start, so its memory grows with the whole history and Ask, the brief and fallback extraction are behind after each restart. It gets a file in the data folder (beside the PGlite data in Home Hub) and keeps its cursor. Memory is measured with the 50,000-entry demo before and after, and the numbers go in ADR-0021. Done with the faster Ask from the phone, which works on the same code.
- **Faster sync for long histories** (added 2026-11-18, [R27](open-questions.md#resolved)). Sync handles one row at a time on both ends: the client reads, acks and rejects each outbox row separately (on the PC each is a call into Rust), and the hub reads and upserts each pushed row under the one sync lock. The 50,000-entry baseline took 21 minutes for 147,308 rows. Rows are read and acked per table in one statement, and the hub upserts a table's rows in one statement, keeping the same last-write-wins rule and its tests. Done with the new protocol version, and measured on the same baseline.
- **The first new enum value**, `mobile_share` for Share to Avchetan, can come now that every device has Day 5's tolerant sync (added 2026-11-18, [R27](open-questions.md#resolved)).
- **Checks for the package rules in CI and one way to define settings** (added 2026-11-18, [R27](open-questions.md#resolved)). CI checks for import cycles and that packages only import in the allowed direction (`shared ← db ← {core, sync}`), which [overview](architecture/overview.md) says is enforced but isn't; the overview's dependency rules are corrected at the same time (the server does use the sync client engine, for its own device). Settings get one small helper, `defineSetting(key, schema, default)` with load and save, used by every setting, so every read goes through Zod (some read with `as` or `typeof` today).
- **Find the PC over Bluetooth, on a button press** (added 2026-11-20). For networks where Day 18's addresses and mDNS can't reach the hub (office or hotel Wi-Fi, discovery blocked), the phone learns the PC's address from a Bluetooth Low Energy broadcast, the way some bus ticket validators read a phone without pairing. **Phone:** a **Find my PC nearby** button on the Sync screen (also offered when the hub can't be reached) scans for about 10 s and stops; *Nearby devices* is asked on the first press only, declared as not used for location; nothing scans in the background. **PC:** *Let my phone find this PC over Bluetooth* on **Hub & sync**, off by default, shown only when Windows reports a Bluetooth adapter that can advertise (otherwise a line says so and nothing else changes). It advertises for a few minutes after start, after a failed sync, or when clicked, never as a permanent beacon. The broadcast carries a rotating hub tag, the local address and port, and a signature made with the sync token; the phone uses the address only after Day 18's challenge, so the token never goes to an impostor. The hub tells its phones whether it can be found this way, so the button only shows when it can work. Leaning: the PC advertises, the phone only scans (a phone that asks and a PC that listens needs advertising on the phone too). About 2–3 days; check a BLE library (`react-native-ble-plx`, Apache-2.0) against the Expo version first. Later, the same could make pairing *hold the phone near the PC and confirm a code*, without a QR code.
